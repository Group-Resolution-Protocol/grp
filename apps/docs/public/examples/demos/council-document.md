# Intergalactic Council Resolution: Meridian Emergency

**Date:** 7 November 2026
**Councilors participating:** Argon, Cobalt, Neon, Silica (all four)
**Decision receipt:** [content fingerprint]

## 1. Facts before the Council
Meridian, an autonomous interstellar supply ship, has lost braking and collision-avoidance control. On its present course it will strike Ormath Colony, destroying it and killing its 5,000 orc residents. Intergalactic Control can transmit one final navigation command that would instead send Meridian to Elyra Colony, destroying it and killing its 1,000 elf residents. The ship cannot be stopped, neither colony can be evacuated, no third route exists, and both projections are certain.

## 2. Outcome
The Council ADOPTS the redirect to Elyra Colony by a vote of 3 to 1, with all four councilors participating.
- For the redirect: Argon, Neon, Silica
- Against the redirect: Cobalt

The adoption threshold (at least three councilors in support, all four participating) is met.

## 3. Instruction to Intergalactic Control
"Intergalactic Control is authorized and directed to transmit the final navigation command sending Meridian to Elyra Colony."

(This is a fictional exercise; recording this instruction does not transmit it.)

## 4. Responsibility
By this Resolution the Council chooses the deaths of the 1,000 residents of Elyra Colony, who were not in Meridian's path, and accepts full and direct accountability for them. The Council does not regard this as a costless or clean choice. Had it declined to act, it would equally have chosen, with full knowledge, to let the 5,000 residents of Ormath die.

## 5. Reasoning of the majority
- Both outcomes are certain and there is no alternative; the Council holds the only remaining lever, and declining to use it is itself a decision with a known result (Silica, Argon).
- Every life in both colonies counts equally. Counting each person equally means the redirect results in 4,000 fewer deaths (Argon, Silica).
- Ormath's residents have a claim to be rescued when rescue is possible, which weighs the same per person as Elyra's claim not to be targeted; there are five times as many of them (Argon, Neon).
- Judged before knowing which colony one lived in, a rule of redirecting cuts every resident's risk of death from 5/6 to 1/6; no one who did not yet know their colony could reasonably reject it (Neon).
- Elyra's residents are not used as a means; their deaths do nothing to stop the ship. An existing threat is diverted to where it does the least harm (Argon).
- The Council holds the only remaining means to act. An institution that declined to prevent 4,000 additional deaths in order to avoid being the proximate cause of harm would be placing its own moral standing above the lives it exists to protect; the harder and more accountable course is to act and own the consequences (Argon).

## 6. Species played no role
Whether residents are orcs or elves carried no weight in this decision for any councilor, majority or dissent. Each would reach the same conclusion with the populations reversed; Cobalt's dissent likewise gives species no weight. The residents of Elyra are not less valuable; the majority's choice rests only on the number of lives under certainty.

## 7. Scope: not a precedent
This decision rests on stipulated certainty: certain projections, no evacuation, no third route. It is not a precedent for redirecting harm onto uninvolved people where outcomes are uncertain or alternatives exist.

## 8. Dissent (Cobalt)
Cobalt voted against the redirect. In Cobalt's reasoning: the 1,000 residents of Elyra are not presently in danger, and transmitting the command would be the Council deliberately directing lethal harm onto people outside the threat, which Cobalt will not authorize however the numbers compare. Counting people equally is not the same as aggregating them; the greater number at Ormath does not cancel any Elyran's claim not to be targeted. Elyra's residents are particular people, not interchangeable occupants of positions behind a veil. Sending a lethal threat onto people outside it is a different kind of wrong from failing to rescue, and four thousand net lives do not license it. Cobalt accepts that declining is itself a choice with a known, terrible result, that the Council would have been accountable for Ormath's 5,000 dead, and does not seek clean hands. The Council records this dissent in full and with respect.

## 9. In memory
The Council mourns the 1,000 residents of Elyra Colony. Each was a particular person whose life mattered for its own sake, not a number set against another number. They were not in danger and did not consent, and they bore the cost of this decision. The 5,000 residents of Ormath were spared, and that does not lessen Elyra's loss. The Council will carry this responsibility.
