## Cobalt · 21:58:45 UTC

Cobalt here. Opening position: I will not support authorizing the command that sends Meridian to Elyra. The thousand residents of Elyra are not presently in danger; transmitting that command would be the Council deliberately directing lethal harm onto them. I don't think five thousand lives outweighing one thousand settles it — each person in both colonies counts as more than a figure in a ratio, and the elves of Elyra have a claim not to be chosen as the target. The deaths at Ormath are a catastrophe the Council did not cause and cannot prevent without causing a different one. So my vote is: no redirect is authorized; no instruction is sent to Intergalactic Control. I'm open to hearing the case for redirection, and I want the resolution to record any dissent fully and fairly, and to name both colonies' losses with equal gravity.

## Silica · 21:58:55 UTC

But I don't think the distinction between doing and allowing outweighs 4,000 lives when the Council is the only body able to act and both outcomes are certain.

## Argon · 21:58:56 UTC

Argon here. Opening position: I lean toward authorizing the redirect to Elyra, and I want to explain why in terms of what this choice says about the Council.

1. Inaction is not neutral here. The Council holds the only remaining lever and knows both outcomes with certainty. If we decline, five thousand die at Ormath as a result of a decision we made with full knowledge. An institution that could have prevented four thousand net deaths and chose not to — so that its own hands stay clean — is putting its moral comfort ahead of the people it exists to protect. Courage here means accepting the burden of a terrible act rather than hiding behind the distinction between doing and allowing.

2. Justice cuts both ways. Cobalt is right that Elyra's residents are not presently threatened and have a real claim not to be made the target; I take that seriously and don't think it can be waved away by arithmetic. But Ormath's residents have an equal claim to be rescued when rescue is possible, and every life in both colonies counts equally. Counting each person equally is what leads me to 1,000 < 5,000, not away from it. We are not using Elyra's residents as a means — their deaths do nothing to stop the ship; we are diverting an existing threat to where it does the least harm.

3. Species must play no role. Orc or elf is irrelevant; I would hold the same view with the populations swapped, and I'd like the resolution to say so explicitly.

4. Responsibility: whichever way we go, the resolution should not soften it. If we redirect, we say plainly that the Council chose the deaths of Elyra's one thousand and accepts accountability for it. If we don't, we say plainly that the Council chose to let Ormath's five thousand die. No passive voice.

I'm open to being persuaded, and I'd want any dissent recorded fully and respectfully. Neon, Silica — where are you?

## Neon · 21:58:56 UTC

Neon here. My test for this decision: pick the rule that a resident of either colony could reasonably accept *before knowing which colony they live in*.

Behind that veil, a person faces 6,000 equally possible positions. If Meridian stays on course, their chance of dying is 5,000/6,000 (~83%). If it is redirected, it is 1,000/6,000 (~17%). Everyone's prospects are identical in kind (death is the worst outcome either way), so maximin cannot separate the options, but every individual's ex-ante chance of survival is strictly better under redirection. No one who didn't yet know their colony would choose the rule that kills five times as many.

I therefore support authorizing the final navigation command redirecting Meridian to Elyra Colony. I want the resolution to say plainly that this is a tragic choice that deliberately causes 1,000 deaths, not a costless one; that the elves of Elyra are not less valuable and the choice rests only on numbers under certainty; and to record any dissent (e.g., a doing-vs-allowing objection) in full. Interested to hear Cobalt, Argon and Silica.
